Two People Disagree About Stalker

A hundred and sixty-one minutes of men walking slowly through a wet field, watched with someone who loves it, by someone who did not.

filmStalkerAndrei Tarkovsky · 1979

Marta programmes a cinema and has seen this film nineteen times. I have now seen it twice, eleven years apart, and disliked it differently on each occasion.

We recorded the argument rather than writing it up, because neither of us was going to concede in prose.I did concede, slightly, at minute fifty-one. It is audible.

Marta Oyelaran

You said last time that nothing happens. I want to start there, because it’s wrong in an interesting way.

The Professor

I said nothing happens for very long stretches, which is different and also true.

Marta Oyelaran

It’s true and it’s the point. The film is about the cost of getting somewhere. If the journey were exciting it would cost nothing.

The Professor

See, this is the move I can never accept. “It’s boring on purpose” is unfalsifiable. Any dull film can claim it.

Marta Oyelaran

It’s not unfalsifiable. You can ask whether the boredom is doing anything. Watch what happens to your attention in the trolley sequence — the four minutes on the rails with no dialogue. By the end of it you’re reading the actors’ faces harder than you were at the start. It trains you.

The Professor

Or it exhausts me and I mistake exhaustion for depth.

Marta Oyelaran

Those are hard to tell apart from the inside. But there’s a test: does it work on a rewatch, when you already know nothing is coming? If it’s just withholding, the second viewing collapses. This one doesn’t.

The Professor

Nineteen times, though.

Marta Oyelaran

Nineteen times.

He has the better rhetorical position here and the worse argument, which is his most familiar condition.

The Professor

Let me try the honest version rather than the clever one. I think I resent films that require a particular mood to land, because I can’t summon that mood on demand and I feel locked out.

Marta Oyelaran

That’s much better than the boring-on-purpose objection.

The Professor

It’s also less flattering to me.

Marta Oyelaran

Usually the way. And I’d say — yes, it requires something. So does a long walk. You wouldn’t say a mountain is badly designed because you have to be in the mood for it.

You wouldn’t say a mountain is badly designed because you have to be in the mood for it.

Marta Oyelaran
The Professor

So where does that leave the recommendation? Somebody reads this and has a free evening.

Marta Oyelaran

Watch it on a night when you have nowhere to be afterwards. Not tired. Not with someone who checks their phone. If those three things aren’t true, wait — it’ll keep.

The Professor

And my version: watch the first forty minutes. If your attention has changed shape by then, stay. If you’re just counting, you have my permission to leave, and Marta’s contempt.

Marta Oyelaran

Not contempt. Disappointment.

No score. The site has an essay three cards away arguing against exactly that, and I am not going to lose an argument to myself in public twice in one month.

On “pours”

One pour is about six minutes — roughly how long a drink lasts if you are paying attention to it. A minute count would be a claim about how fast you read, made by someone who has never met you.
