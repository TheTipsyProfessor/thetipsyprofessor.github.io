The dryers in this place cost one euro for eleven minutes, which is a strange unit, and everyone standing in front of them has the expression of someone doing arithmetic they resent. I was there because I had packed for four days and stayed nine.

Two women were folding on the table behind me. I could not see them for most of it. I want to be honest that what follows is reconstructed --- I wrote it down on the walk back, in the dark, and I have certainly improved the timing.

::: pull
"He is not a bad man. He is a man who has never once been bored in the company of his own thoughts, and it has made him very tiring."
--- overheard, folding a duvet cover
:::

The other one said nothing for a while. Then: *é o mesmo, não é.*^[Roughly: "it's the same thing, isn't it." I have been told my ear for Portuguese is generous to itself.] And they went on folding.

## Why this is the good stuff

I have sat through a great many organised cultural experiences. Guided walks. A cooking class in which eight adults were praised for chopping. A fado night with a laminated menu explaining fado. All of them were fine. None of them told me anything I could not have read.

Forty minutes in a laundromat told me that in this city you can say something devastating about your husband to a woman you have known for six weeks, and she will let it sit, and you will both keep folding.^[Compare the version of this conversation that happens in a bar, where somebody would have had to *respond*. The laundromat has the enormous advantage of giving everyone something to do with their hands.] That is a fact about a place. You cannot buy a ticket for it.

::: aside
The professor would like it noted that he was also doing his laundry, and was not loitering. This is technically true and entirely beside the point.
:::

## The rules I have arrived at

The ethics here are not nothing, and I have thought about them more than the tone of this piece suggests.

- Public place, ordinary volume, no names. If somebody lowers their voice, the conversation is over as far as I am concerned.
- Never a language the speakers assume nobody around them has. That one is not eavesdropping, it is trespassing.
- Change enough that they would not recognise themselves, and nothing that would change what was meant.
- If it only works because it is cruel, it does not work.

That last one disqualifies about half of what I write down.

I keep a small collection of these --- long, static, pointless recordings of rooms. Not for the content. For the reminder that the content is almost never the point.

## What I actually keep

Not the good lines. The good lines are easy and they are usually the least true part of an evening --- people perform for each other constantly, even in a laundromat, especially in a laundromat.

What I keep is the pause. The six or seven seconds where the second woman decided not to argue. Every real conversation I have overheard in fifteen years of doing this has turned on a silence, and no guided walk has ever once been silent.

> Culture is not the thing a place puts on for you. It is what the place does when the wash is nearly done and nobody has anywhere else to be.

My dryer stopped. I had two euros left and I spent them both on eleven minutes I did not need.
