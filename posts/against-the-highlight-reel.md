Everybody already agrees that the highlight reel is dishonest. That agreement has cost nobody anything and changed nothing, which is how you can tell it is not really a belief.

I want to make the more annoying argument: the highlight reel is not dishonest. Every individual item in it is true. That is precisely the problem, and it is why knowing about it does not help.^[This is the same structure as a misleading statistic. You cannot defend yourself by fact-checking the components. The deception is in the selection, and selection leaves no fingerprints.]

## The honest ledger

Here is a week I posted about, beside the same week as it was.

| What went up | Duration | What it was next to |
|---|---|---|
| The lake at six in the morning | 20 min | Four hours of not being able to sleep |
| A very good plate of food | 45 min | Eating alone and minding, a bit |
| Two friends laughing | 3 sec | An argument that had finished ten minutes earlier |
| "Finally finished the essay" | 1 sec | Eleven days of not finishing the essay |

Nothing in the left column is false. Everything in the left column omits its denominator.

::: pull
A life is not a sequence of moments. It is a ratio, and we have built an entire vocabulary for reporting only the numerator.
:::

## Where my own argument falls apart

The obvious next move is to demand the denominator. Post the four hours of insomnia. Write the eleven days. Be *real*, in the way that has itself become a genre with its own lighting.

I do not believe this works, and here is where I lose the argument I came in with.

::: aside
Note that he has just spent two hundred words establishing a position and is now going to spend four hundred dismantling it. He does this constantly and calls it rigour.
:::

Nobody experiences their own life as a ratio either. Memory is *also* a highlight reel --- a much older and much less honest one, running on hardware that has been discarding denominators since long before anyone had an audience. When I remember Reykjavík I do not remember nineteen hours of tiredness. I remember a thermos. The editing happened inside my head, for free, and it was more aggressive than anything I would do in public.

So the charge cannot be "you edited." Everyone edits, always, including alone, including in private, including in the diary nobody will read.

## What the charge actually is

It is not editing. It is **editing toward enviability**, which is a specific and unusual choice of objective function, and one that no memory has ever made on its own.

Your memory edits toward *significance* --- it keeps the thermos because the thermos was strange, not because the thermos was impressive. Left alone, a mind builds a record organised around what surprised it. Pointed at an audience, the same mind rebuilds the record around what would be envied. Those two archives barely overlap, and living inside the second one for long enough will genuinely change what you go and do.

That is the harm, and it survives everybody knowing about it, because it does not operate at the level of any individual post.

> The question is not whether you are telling the truth. It is what you have quietly started optimising for while telling it.

## What I do about it

Not much, honestly, and I distrust anyone with a five-point programme here.

One thing only: every essay on this site carries the city it was written in and the time on the clock when it was finished. Not because that is interesting --- it is not --- but because it is the one piece of metadata I cannot edit toward enviability. `02:14` is not impressive. `02:14` is just what it says.

It is a very small denominator. It is the only one I have found that does not immediately become a performance of having a denominator.
