I have read the literature. I know about the blue light and the cool room and the consistent hour. I am not asking for help. This is an inventory, and inventories are the only genre in which I am reliably honest.

1. **Re-read the last good paragraph I wrote.** Never the whole piece. Just the one paragraph, four or five times, the way you press a bruise.
2. **Plan a trip I will not take.** Currently: the sleeper from Belgrade to Bar, which I have now planned eleven times and costed to the euro.
3. **Look up the population of somewhere I passed through.** Tonight: Ptuj, Slovenia. Twenty-three thousand. I have no use for this.
4. **Rewrite an email I sent in 2019.** In my head, obviously. It is much better now.
5. **Establish that it is a reasonable hour somewhere.** It is always a reasonable hour somewhere. This is the single most enabling fact in geography.^[The professor is aware that this reasoning also justifies drinking at breakfast, and has declined to examine the connection.]
6. Make a list very much like this one, on paper, and lose it.
7. **Argue with something I read nine hours ago.** Out loud, occasionally. I have a small flat and thin walls and I have made peace with what my neighbours think.
8. **Check whether a specific song is as good as I remember.** It is either exactly as good or completely ruined, never in between, and I can never predict which.
9. Stand in front of the open fridge with no intention of eating.
10. **Work out how long until the first coffee.** See [the second-cup theorem](/essay/the-second-cup-theorem/), which does not apply at this hour and which I consult anyway.
11. **Read one page of a language textbook.** :ja[その晩、] --- and then stop, because a page is a lie, it is always one line.
12. Reorganise the photographs from a trip I took in March. No deletions. There are never deletions.
13. **Compose the opening sentence of something I will not write.** I have hundreds of these. They are the best sentences I own, and every one of them is load-bearing for nothing.
14. **Find the thing I lost earlier, at last, in the obvious place.** Three in the morning is unreasonably good at this and I do not know why.
15. Decide, firmly, to fix the sleeping.
16. **Look up flights.** Not to book. Just to see that they exist and are going.
17. **Get up, make the coffee at four, and treat the whole night as an early start.** This is the one that works, in the sense that it ends the problem, and not at all in the sense that it solves it.

::: aside
Items 15 and 17 have appeared in every version of this list since 2017, in that order, and he has never once noticed the contradiction while awake.
:::

::: pull
A habit you have chosen is a routine. A habit that has chosen you is a schedule, and you are on it.
:::

The advice all assumes you want the night to end. Some of it, most nights, I do. But there is a particular quality to four in the morning in a city that is not yours --- somebody's scooter three streets over, the air finally at a temperature a person can stand --- and I have never been able to argue myself into wanting to have slept through it.

> Every one of these is a way of being awake on purpose, which is a different condition entirely from being unable to sleep, and the difference is worth about four hours a night to me.

Number 17. It is 03:41. The kettle is close enough.
