The illustrations on this site are of me, and I did not commission a single one of them. That is not modesty. It is the setup for something I have been trying to say honestly for about eight months and keep getting wrong.

A friend started drawing me on trips. Not as a gift — as a way of not being bored in the same places I was not bored in. After the fourth or fifth I asked for them, and after the twelfth I started putting them here, and somewhere in there the pictures stopped being a record of where I'd been and became the way I look.^[I have a specific memory of catching my reflection in a window in Ljubljana and being briefly disappointed that it was less well composed. That is the sentence this whole essay exists to confess.]

## The first thing, which is boring

Yes: I look different in them. Everyone does. The drawn version of a person is edited toward legibility — the jaw a little more decided, the posture better than the posture was.

Nobody needs an essay about that. It is the same observation as the mirror, the camera, the recording of your own voice, and it has been made better by everyone who has ever made it.

::: pull
The interesting thing is not that the picture flatters you. It is that you begin to pose for a picture nobody is currently taking.
:::

## The second thing

About six months in, I noticed I had started standing differently in places I found beautiful.

Not for a camera. There was no camera. I had simply learned, from a year of being drawn, what *being a man looking at a thing* looks like from outside — and somewhere below the level I get a vote on, I had started producing it.

::: aside
He tested this. He stood at a viewpoint in Sintra and tried to look at it without arranging himself. He reports that he could not, and that trying made it worse.
:::

This is a genuinely strange thing to discover about yourself and I do not think it is rare. It is the mechanism behind everything I complained about in [the highlight reel](/essay/against-the-highlight-reel/), arriving from the opposite direction: I had argued there that an audience changes what you go and do. It turns out an audience also changes what you *are* while doing it, and it does not need to be present, and it does not need to exist.

## What I decided, which is not a solution

I kept publishing them.

The honest defence is thin but I think it holds: the drawings are the only part of this site that does not pretend to be a record. Nobody looks at an ink wash of a man in a papaya field and thinks *this happened exactly like this*. The prose makes that claim constantly and gets away with it because prose is better at lying quietly.

> An illustration announces that it is a version. A photograph pretends it is the thing. Of the two, I have come to trust the one that admits what it's doing.

There is a plate on this page of me looking off to the right at nothing in particular. I was, in fact, looking at a bus timetable, and I was annoyed. Both versions are true. Only one of them is any good, and I am not sure that reflects well on any of us.
